## Some designs and Design System Variants

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:2940/1*zBGs3iWfFcTK9W0__ns_1g.png)English LTR Designs![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:2940/1*6lokMV6PDkKCEIOxDW8amw.png)Arabic RTL Designs![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:2914/1*xu0Ru2b6Jsd1UpSYUlLtwQ.png)Atoms and Molecules with Arabic Variants

Just like many other Designers from around the world, I was raised up designing digital experiences in English only. We get so habitual to designing in English or other left to right languages that we tend to consider that all the designs are supposed to in a certain manner. Let’s understand this with an example.

Press enter or click to view image in full size

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:700/1*WsY19z8vplSLhpnOoBZZRA.png)

Dubizzle: English and Arabic Design

1. Top Bar: The notification icon is shifted to left side of the screen
2. Search Bar: The icon shifted from left side to right side
3. Carousel: The cards horizontal scroll shifted from right to left.
4. Bottom Nav: The icons are shifted completely

## So is it simply the mirror of Left to Right Design?

Most of the people simply say that the Arabic Design is nothing but a simple mirror of Left to Right design. But the answer is not that simple

![](https://miro.medium.com/v2/resize:fit:480/0*9AA-CKg1q1KTmFz3.gif)

Yes, the alignment majorly shifts in mirror form which is pretty logical because the flow of reading is not left to right but right to left. But not all the things change.
